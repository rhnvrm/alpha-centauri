# Lore and setting

## The real sky

Alpha Centauri is not one star. It is a triple system.

Alpha Centauri A and B are a close pair. They orbit one another roughly once
every 80 years. Proxima Centauri, also called Alpha Centauri C, is a much
smaller red dwarf on a far wider orbit. Proxima is the closest individual star
to the Sun, while Alpha Centauri A and B are the nearest Sun-like pair.

The International Astronomical Union gives Alpha Centauri A the traditional
name **Rigil Kentaurus** and Proxima Centauri its name. Alpha Centauri B does
not currently have a separate proper name in that system. In the game, people
still use "Alpha Centauri" for the whole system and "A/B" for the pair around
which Aurora orbits.

This gives the setting a sky with more structure than a generic science-fiction
starfield. A colony near the A/B pair can have two bright suns whose positions
change over the course of an 80-year stellar orbit. Proxima is a distant red
point, not a second nearby sun. Its frequent flares are a reminder that the
system is not a quiet, friendly neighborhood.

Sources: [NASA on the Alpha Centauri triple system](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/other-stars-other-worlds/our-nearest-celestial-neighbor-an-exotic-3-star-system/), [NASA on Proxima Centauri](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/proxima-centauri/), and the [IAU naming report](https://www.iau.org/static/science/scientific_bodies/working_groups/280/wg-starnames-triennial-report-2015-2018.pdf).

## The fictional move

Aurora is invented. No habitable colony planet is being claimed, and the game
does not present its terrain, ecology, or resources as discoveries from
astronomy. The real system supplies the distance, the light, and the awkward
three-body setting. The colony supplies the human problem.

The fleet reaches Aurora in the Alpha Centauri A/B system in 2247. Earth can
send a message, but the message takes 4.37 years to arrive. A report takes
another 4.37 years to return. That delay is the setting's central physical
fact and the source of the game's politics.

## Why Aurora is worth governing

Aurora is not a blank planet waiting for a city. It has a native ecology that
the first settlers do not understand. Wetlands, microbial mats, seasonal
floods, and unfamiliar resource networks make a fixed Earth plan unreliable.

The colony's first machines are practical rather than heroic: surveyors,
construction robots, relay equipment, habitats, power systems, reservoirs,
greenhouses, roads, and cargo infrastructure. The drama comes from decisions
made with incomplete information, not from combat or a monster hidden behind
the next ridge.

## Daneel's place in the story

R. Daneel Olivaw is the fleet's Colonial Steward. The name is a private
homage, not a claim that the game adapts Isaac Asimov's fiction. Daneel's
dialogue, decisions, and relationship with the Administrator are original.

He lives with the colony. Earth does not send him a faster channel or a secret
view of the future. He receives delivered instructions, reads the current
local state, and acts through the tools the page exposes. His reports are also
selective and delayed. He must decide what Earth needs to know, just as Earth
must decide what Daneel needs to understand.

That is why the lore leads directly into the mechanics:

```text
the farther away the colony is
→ the less useful fixed commands become
→ the more important local judgment becomes
→ the more political weight delegation carries
```

## The three letters

The missions are three stages in the relationship between Earth and Aurora.

**The First Light** asks whether Earth can give a command that survives an
outdated map.

**The Meaning of Enough** asks whether Earth can state a goal clearly enough
for another intelligence to pursue it without guessing at its values.

**The Right to Decide** asks how long a distant administration can remain in
charge when every correction arrives years too late.

The colony becomes a place rather than a project. The final question is not
whether Daneel can obey. It is what Earth still has the right to decide.

## Lore guardrails

- Keep Aurora, its ecology, its colony history, and its resources explicitly fictional.
- Use real astronomy for distance, stellar structure, light delay, and naming.
- Do not imply that current observations confirm a habitable planet around Alpha Centauri A or B.
- Keep the two-sun sky as a visual and cultural feature, not a promise of an exact orbital simulation.
- Write original Daneel dialogue. Do not quote Asimov or reuse film, game, or book assets.
- Let scientific uncertainty create choices. Do not use unexplained alien lore to bypass the simulation's rules.
